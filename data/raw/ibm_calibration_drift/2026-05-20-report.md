# Statistical analysis of calibration drift in IBM Quantum Heron processors and association with environmental indicators

**Report date:** 2026-05-20
**Observation window:** 2026-01-31 to 2026-05-20 (109 days)
**Dataset:** `phanerozoic/qiskit-calibration-drift`, version of 2026-05-20, 5,295,956 calibration records across 31 fields.

## 1. Scope and aims

This report presents a statistical analysis of the `qiskit-calibration-drift` dataset, covering all records collected since the dataset was first populated. The dataset contains snapshots of `BackendProperties` for four IBM Quantum Heron processors at IBM's Yorktown Heights, NY facility, joined to time-aligned ambient meteorological and space-weather indicators. The processors comprised three active Heron r2 devices (`ibm_fez`, `ibm_kingston`, `ibm_marrakesh`; 156 qubits each) and one retired Heron r1 device (`ibm_torino`, 133 qubits, retired 2026-04-27). Collection cadence is documented as 30 minutes by the upstream GitHub Action, although the IBM API's `last_update_date` (`snapshot_update_time` in the dataset) advances at a slower, irregular rate.

The analysis addresses four questions in sequence:

1. What are the marginal distributions of each calibration property family, and what is their long-term temporal structure?
2. How are calibration parameters distributed across the qubit population on a given device, and how do the four devices compare?
3. Is there evidence of association between calibration parameters and ambient or space-weather indicators?
4. What is the measurable effect of chip-wide recalibration events on the population distribution of error metrics?

## 2. Methods

Analyses were performed in Python 3.13 with `polars` 1.39 for data manipulation, `scipy` 1.17, `statsmodels` 0.14, and `scikit-learn` 1.8 for statistical procedures.

Records with `is_failure_ceiling == True` were excluded from descriptive and inferential analyses, following the dataset documentation. The `T1` and `T2` columns were normalized to microseconds across two unit conventions observed in the data: records with `unit == "us"` retained their native scale; records with `unit IS NULL` for these families were multiplied by 10⁶ to convert from seconds, on the basis of a clear bimodal scale in the raw `value` column whose boundary coincided with a documented schema transition in mid-May 2026.

The following procedures were applied:

- *Descriptive statistics.* Mean, standard deviation, median, percentiles (1, 5, 25, 50, 75, 95, 99), skewness, and excess kurtosis per (backend, property family) tuple.
- *Cadence.* Inter-snapshot gap distributions per backend and per family using the differences of `snapshot_update_time` and of `calibrated_time` conditioned on value change.
- *Stationarity and trend.* Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) and Kwiatkowski-Phillips-Schmidt-Shin (KPSS) tests on per-(backend, qubit) and per-(backend, pair) error time series; Mann-Kendall trend test with tie correction; Hurst exponent via rescaled-range (R/S) analysis with log-spaced window sizes.
- *Periodicity.* Lomb-Scargle periodogram on irregularly-sampled per-qubit series, plus autocorrelation function (ACF) at lags 1 and 24, and Engle's ARCH-LM test on first differences.
- *Heavy tails.* Hill tail-index estimator at varying upper fractions (k/n ∈ {0.005, 0.010, 0.020, 0.050}); peaks-over-threshold counts at 3×, 5×, 10×, 30×, and 100× the family-wide median.
- *Heteroskedasticity.* Breusch-Pagan and White tests on residuals of linear regression of value on time index, per qubit series.
- *Environmental association.* Per-(backend, snapshot_update_time, family) aggregation to the snapshot median, joined to per-snapshot environmental indicators. Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlations against 14 environmental variables, with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction at α = 0.05. Partial Spearman correlation conditioning on a linear time index, computed via the rank-residual approach. Decile stress comparison via Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Cohen's d on the top and bottom 10% of each environmental indicator. Lagged Pearson cross-correlation on doubly-detrended series at lags up to ±20 snapshots. Granger causality F-test on first-differenced bivariate series at lags 1 through 4.
- *Multivariate structure.* Principal component analysis on the 14-variable environmental block (standardized). Mutual information regression and Random Forest feature importance for snapshot median error as a function of environment plus a time index, with comparison against time-only baselines (ΔR²).
- *Recalibration impact.* Mann-Whitney U comparison of error medians 12 hours pre-event and 12 hours post-event, conditioned on the property families annotated as touched by each chip-wide event.

## 3. Data-quality observations

Three properties of the dataset bear directly on any downstream use and were established as a precondition to the analyses that follow.

*Mixed units in coherence columns.* Records of `T1` and `T2` are stored under two unit conventions in the dataset. Rows with `unit IS NULL` carry values consistent with the SI base unit (seconds); rows with `unit == "us"` carry values in microseconds. The two conventions partition cleanly by `observed_time`: NULL-unit rows precede 2026-05-07, "us" rows follow 2026-05-13, with no temporal overlap. Normalization to microseconds yields a unimodal physical distribution (median T1 in the range 140-255 µs across the four devices); without normalization, summary statistics are dominated by the unit boundary.

*Identity among single-qubit gate-error families.* For every (backend, snapshot_update_time, qubit) tuple in which more than one of `sx_error`, `x_error`, `id_error`, and `rx_error` was reported, all reported values were identical to floating-point precision. Across 18,090 such tuples the maximum absolute pairwise difference was zero. The dataset documentation states that the four basis gates are "typically" bit-identical; in this version of the dataset that relationship is exact in every case. The `rz_error` column is identically zero in all 137,592 of its records, consistent with virtual-Z (frame-change) implementation.

*Readout quantization.* The columns `readout_error`, `prob_meas0_prep1`, and `prob_meas1_prep0` lie on a grid of multiples of 1/8192 ≈ 1.221 × 10⁻⁴ in 99.94% of records, with the remaining 0.06% located in rows with `unit == ""` (older schema). The columns `measure_gate_error` and `measure_2_error` are on the same grid in 100% of records. This is consistent with readout-fidelity estimation drawing on exactly 2¹³ shots.

These observations are reported here rather than in a separate methodology section because each affects the interpretation of the marginal statistics presented below.

## 5. Calibration cadence

The median time between distinct values of `snapshot_update_time` is 93 minutes on `ibm_kingston`, 308 minutes on `ibm_fez`, and 316 minutes on `ibm_marrakesh`. The `ibm_torino` snapshots in the dataset share a single update timestamp.

The median inter-recalibration gap (between successive `calibrated_time` values where `value` changes) varies by family. For `T1`, `T2`, `sx_error`, `cz_error`, and `rzz_error` the median gap is 24.5-25.1 hours on every active device, consistent with a daily recalibration schedule. For `readout_error`, `prob_meas0_prep1`, and `prob_meas1_prep0` the median gap is 3.6-3.8 hours. Maximum observed gaps reach 1,965 hours (≈82 days) for one `cz_error` pair on `ibm_marrakesh`.

Twenty-seven distinct chip-wide recalibration events (`chipwide_recal_event_id` non-null) are recorded across the three active devices. Each event affects 156 qubits with a single exception (one event on `ibm_kingston` affecting two qubits). Inter-event gaps on each device cluster around 24 hours.

## 6. Temporal structure of error metrics

Per-qubit and per-pair error time series of length at least 50 were analyzed for stationarity, monotone trend, and long-memory properties. A random subsample of up to 300 series per family was used for tractability. Results are reported in Table 2.

| Family | n_series | ADF p<0.05 | KPSS p<0.05 | MK p<0.05 | MK positive | Hurst median | ACF(1) median | ARCH-LM p<0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1            | 148 | 0.87 | 0.28 | 0.37 | 0.28 | 0.66 | 0.12 | 0.41 |
| T2            | 154 | 0.92 | 0.24 | 0.28 | 0.29 | 0.66 | 0.09 | 0.62 |
| sx_error      | 200 | 0.93 | 0.34 | 0.83 | 0.58 | 0.80 | 0.95 | 0.09 |
| cz_error      | 200 | 0.88 | 0.42 | 0.83 | 0.55 | 0.87 | 0.96 | 0.14 |
| readout_error | 200 | 0.85 | 0.49 | 0.74 | 0.66 | 0.71 | 0.28 | 0.97 |

*Table 2: Per-series rejection rates of the indicated nulls. "MK positive" is the fraction of all series (not the fraction of rejecting series) with positive Mann-Kendall z. Hurst exponent estimated via rescaled-range with log-spaced window sizes.*

Three features emerge. First, all four error metrics yield median Hurst exponents above the random-walk threshold of 0.5; for `cz_error` the value of 0.87 is well into long-memory territory. Second, the ADF and KPSS tests yield apparently inconsistent rejection rates for most families, a pattern characteristic of trend-stationary processes rather than unit-root non-stationarity. Third, the Mann-Kendall test detects an upward monotone trend in 55-66% of `sx_error`, `cz_error`, and `readout_error` series, and a downward trend in only 25-33% of those series, indicating a population-level upward drift over the observation window. Coherence times (T1, T2) do not show this asymmetry.

Lomb-Scargle periodograms on the same series do not show a clean diurnal mode. The fraction of series whose peak period falls within 2 hours of 24 hours is 12% for T1, 6% for T2, and below 1% for `sx_error` and `cz_error`. A weekly band centered at 168 hours captures the peak periodogram power in 23% of `sx_error` series and 15% of `cz_error` series. Power on time scales longer than 200 hours dominates the periodograms of all four error families. ACF at lag 1 is 0.95 for `sx_error` and 0.96 for `cz_error`, reflecting daily recalibration combined with persistent calibration values between recalibrations. The ARCH-LM test rejects homoskedasticity in 97% of `readout_error` series and 62% of `T2` series, indicating volatility clustering in the residuals of those metrics. The Breusch-Pagan and White tests on linear-detrended residuals reject homoskedasticity in 75% and 94% of `sx_error` series respectively and at similar rates for `cz_error`.

## 7. Tail behavior

Hill tail-index estimates of the right tail of each family's value distribution (k = 0.005·n) yield α = 1/ξ values of 16.0 for T1, 7.6 for T2, and 1.2-4.2 for `sx_error`, `cz_error`, `rzz_error`, and `readout_error`. Values of α below 2 imply potentially infinite second moments. The fraction of records with value exceeding 3× the family median is 17.1% for `readout_error`, 9.0% for `cz_error`, 9.0% for `rzz_error`, 5.6% for `sx_error`, 2.1% for T2, and 0.02% for T1. The fraction exceeding 30× the median is 1.0% for `readout_error` and 0.77% for `cz_error`.

### 8.3 Granger causality and lagged cross-correlation

Granger causality F-tests at lags 1 through 4 were applied to first-differenced bivariate series for six selected (backend, family, indicator) pairs identified by the marginal analysis. The pair (`ibm_kingston`, `readout_error`, `dst_nt`) yielded the smallest F-test p-value (p = 0.013 at lag 3). Of the kingston cadence, lag 3 corresponds to approximately 4-5 hours. The other five pairs (`ibm_kingston`/`prob_meas0_prep1` against `Ap_daily` and `kp_index`; `ibm_fez`/`prob_meas0_prep1` and `ibm_fez`/`readout_error` against `Ap_daily`; `ibm_fez`/`readout_error` against `kp_index`) yielded p ≥ 0.37 at every tested lag. The Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold over 24 (six pairs × four lags) tests is p < 0.0021, which the `dst_nt` result does not meet.

Lagged Pearson cross-correlations on doubly-detrended series (after removal of a linear trend in both error and indicator) yielded peak correlations at non-zero lag for several pressure-error pairs. On `ibm_kingston`, `prob_meas1_prep0` versus `pressure_hpa` peaked at lag +14 snapshots with r = -0.61, indicating the pressure series leads the error series. The interpretation of leading-pressure correlations as physical is not supported by the present analysis; the result is reported descriptively.

### 8.5 Multivariate analysis

The second (16.9%) loads positively on `temperature_c`, `pressure_hpa`, `neutron_flux` and negatively on `solar_zenith_deg`, `humidity_pct`. ## 9. Chip-wide recalibration events

For each of the 27 chip-wide events identified by non-null `chipwide_recal_event_id`, the median value of each touched error family in the 12 hours immediately preceding and the 12 hours immediately following the event timestamp was compared via Mann-Whitney U. Across 34 (event, family) pairs that met the minimum sample-size criterion of 30 records per side, the median post/pre ratio ranged from 0.918 to 1.245, with median lift between -8.2% and +24.5%. No (event, family) pair reached p < 0.05 at the available sample sizes (n_pre and n_post between 35 and 627). Aggregating across events by family, the median ratios are 0.97 for `prob_meas1_prep0`, 0.97 for `rzz_error`, 0.98 for `cz_error`, 1.00 for `readout_error`, 1.01 for `prob_meas0_prep1`, 1.05 for `sx_error`, and 1.05 for `T2`. Where directional effect can be assessed at all, chip-wide recalibration events do not produce a detectable shift in the population-level distribution of error metrics in the 12-hour post-event window.

## 10. Discussion

Three findings of the present analysis are stable across procedural variations and bear summary statement.

First, the four-month observation window contains a population-level upward drift in single-qubit and two-qubit error metrics across all three active Heron r2 devices. Mann-Kendall trend tests rejected the null of no monotone trend in 83% of `sx_error` and `cz_error` series with positive-trend fractions of 55-58%; the same test rejected in 74% of `readout_error` series with a positive-trend fraction of 66%. Hurst exponents above 0.5 in every family examined indicate that this drift is not the cumulative effect of independent increments but exhibits long-memory persistence, with H = 0.87 for `cz_error` placing it well into the regime associated with 1/f-type processes. Coherence times (T1, T2) do not exhibit the same population-level upward trend over the window.

Second, decile stress comparisons against the geomagnetic indices `Ap_daily`, `Kp`, and `Dst` indicate moderately-to-largely elevated readout-family error medians during the most disturbed days relative to the most quiet days, with effect sizes (Cohen's d) ranging from 1.5 to 9.7. However, marginal correlations between snapshot-median error and several of these indicators (notably `kp_index`, `Ap_daily`, and `temperature_c` on `ibm_kingston`) do not survive partial-correlation conditioning on a linear time index, and are consistent with shared trend rather than direct association in the present window. The marginal correlation between `dst_nt` and readout-family errors on `ibm_kingston` does survive partial-correlation conditioning at |ρ_partial| ≈ 0.36-0.39 and p < 1.3 × 10⁻³. A Granger causality F-test from `dst_nt` to `ibm_kingston` `readout_error` on first-differenced series reaches p = 0.013 at lag 3 (approximately 5 hours on this device's cadence); this result does not survive Bonferroni correction for 24 tests but constitutes the only Granger-significant pair examined. In multivariate regression, environmental indicators contribute 6-13 percentage points of additional in-sample R² over a time-only baseline for the three readout families on `ibm_marrakesh`, and 0.3-2.0 percentage points on `ibm_kingston` and `ibm_fez`. The dominant environmental feature in the kingston Random Forests is `dst_nt`.

Third, no statistically significant association between cosmic-ray neutron flux at the NMDB Newark station and any error metric was detected in this dataset at the decile level or in marginal/partial correlation analyses. The observed sign was negative in the marginal analysis (errors slightly lower under high neutron flux) but did not reach significance at any threshold. This negative result is compatible with several non-exclusive interpretations: ground-level neutron monitor counts at Newark are an imperfect proxy for the cosmic-ray flux at the chip in Yorktown Heights; reported cosmic-ray-induced quasiparticle bursts in superconducting qubits occur on millisecond timescales and would be averaged out by the dataset's 30-minute polling and chip-wide median aggregation; or local shielding suppresses the soft component of the cosmic-ray flux sufficiently to render the residual coupling undetectable at present sample sizes.

The finding that chip-wide recalibration events do not measurably shift the population-level distribution of error metrics in a 12-hour window deserves separate comment. The sample sizes (typically 150-600 calibrations per side) are sufficient to detect Mann-Whitney effect sizes substantially smaller than those observed. The absence of detectable effect is consistent with a model in which per-qubit calibration drift is statistically stationary around a fixed mean over short windows and the recalibration procedure re-anchors individual qubits but does not change the population-level dispersion. A test of this interpretation requires per-qubit pre/post comparisons rather than population-level comparisons; this is not pursued here.

## 11. Limitations

Several constraints on the inferences drawn here merit acknowledgment.

The observation window of 109 days is short relative to typical geomagnetic-activity variability. Decile stress comparisons against `dst_nt` rely on only 8 storm-decile observations on each backend. Effect-size estimates at the decile level have wide confidence intervals.

Partial-correlation conditioning on a single linear time index removes only the linearly-projected portion of any common trend. The true aging trajectory of each chip is unlikely to be exactly linear. A per-qubit aging spline or a state-space decomposition would be more conservative; such an analysis would also clarify whether the apparent `dst_nt` coupling on `ibm_kingston` is concentrated in a subpopulation of magnetically-susceptible qubits or distributed across the device.

The dataset aggregates calibrations as reported by IBM's API. It does not contain raw single-shot readout data, gate-level pulse calibrations, or device-internal diagnostic timestamps. Any coupling mechanism operating on timescales shorter than the polling cadence (30 minutes) or shorter than IBM's calibration cycle (3.7 hours for readout, 24 hours for coherence and gate fidelity) is not directly observable in this dataset.

The retired `ibm_torino` device contributes a small subset of records (381,435 of 5,295,956, 7.2%) and is included in the analysis only for the descriptive comparisons in Section 4. The polling cadence on `ibm_torino` produces a single `snapshot_update_time` value, which prevents environmental-association analysis on that device.
